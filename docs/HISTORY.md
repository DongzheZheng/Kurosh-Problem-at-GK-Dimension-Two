# Background and references

An associative algebra is **algebraic** when each element satisfies a nonzero polynomial over its base field. It is **nil** when each element is nilpotent. Kurosh's 1941 problem asks whether an algebraic algebra generated by finitely many elements must be finite-dimensional [1]. Golod's construction, using the Golod–Shafarevich inequality, supplied infinite-dimensional nil examples in 1964 [2, 3]. Their exponential growth prompted the polynomial-growth question.

Growth counts the dimension spanned by words of bounded length. Its polynomial exponent is the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension. Bergman's gap theorem excludes dimensions strictly between one and two; Small–Stafford–Warfield prove that affine algebras of GK dimension one satisfy polynomial identities [4, 5]. Kaplansky's finiteness theorem for algebraic PI algebras makes two the smallest GK dimension of an infinite-dimensional affine algebraic example [6]. Shirshov's height theorem supplies a combinatorial proof of this finiteness result [17].

In Section 6 of their 2012 paper [10], Bell–Small–Smoktunowicz formulate **Question 5**, seeking infinite-dimensional affine algebraic algebras of finite GK dimension over uncountable fields, and **Question 6**, seeking GK dimension exactly two. Greenfeld's 2025 introduction records both questions [16]. This repository proves the critical-dimension existence statement over every field, with nilpotence preserved by all field extensions and finite matrix sizes.

Zelmanov's quantitative Kurosh conjecture asks for every realizable nonlinear algebra growth type to be realized by a nil algebra, up to asymptotic equivalence [13, 16].

## Timeline

| Year | Development |
| --- | --- |
| 1941 | Kurosh poses his question [1]. |
| 1964 | Golod constructs infinite-dimensional finitely generated nil algebras from the Golod–Shafarevich method [2, 3]. |
| 1978–1985 | Bergman and Small–Stafford–Warfield establish the low-growth threshold [4, 5]. |
| 2007 | Lenagan–Smoktunowicz obtain polynomially bounded nil examples over countable fields, with GK dimension at most twenty [7]. |
| 2011–2012 | Bell–Young obtain arbitrary-field superpolynomial envelopes; Lenagan–Smoktunowicz–Young lower the countable-field GK bound to three [8, 9]. |
| 2013 | Smoktunowicz–Young obtain quadratic-growth radical algebras over countable algebraically closed fields; nilness was unresolved. Smoktunowicz–Bartholdi construct prime non-nil radical algebras of GK dimension two over countable fields [11, 12]. |
| 2017–2019 | Alahmadi–Alsulami–Jain–Zelmanov develop matrix wreath products and realize every real GK dimension at least eight by countable-field nil algebras [13, 14]. |
| 2023 | Greenfeld–Zelmanov advance nil-algebra growth realization through wreath products [15]. |
| 2025 | Greenfeld improves realization bounds and extends exact nil GK dimensions over countable fields to every real value at least six [16]. |
| 2026 | The construction formalized here gives two-generated, absolutely stably nil algebras of GK dimension two over every field. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.14257.34406). |
